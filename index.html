about

I am a visual artist

The main concepts around my work are sustainability, rituality, tradition, and playfulness. I create abstract forms combining colour, shapes, and rhythm. With “the seriousness of a child at play,” I choose leftover pieces of fabric and other materials, reclaiming what others deem waste, and transform them into a language of renewal, softness, and resilience - creating new forms in which vulnerability itself becomes a form of resistance.

Ό,τι θυμόμαστε μένει | solo exhibition

"The process begins with the creation of a semi-fictional, colorful herbarium. With the seriousness of a child at play, beads, old pieces of fabric and threads are gathered. The design is transferred. The fabric is cut, sewn, and selectively padded. Gestures are repeated as time slows down, liberated from the imperatives of productivity. Neglected materials gain new lives and the tactile experience reactivates, through memory, fragments of a world that has passed and of a future that never came to be". Excerpt from Marina Demosthenous' text on the exhibition.

continuous thread

Continuous Thread Archive →

'Continuous thread' is a socially engaged installation that has been presented in different ways from 2016 to 2023.

Modus Operandi 17, Displacement As a State of Mind, Koraï, CY

Guests' living room, THOC Apothikes Theatre, CY (Photo: Kyriakos Christodoulides)

Animart Greece 2017, Amfissa

International Conference: Protect and Secure Worship, 2023, Chateau Status, Nicosia

In 2016, I created this installation using crochet jute twine and branches. Crochet is a practice learned from my grandmothers which is very common in the Eastern Mediterranean and it is deeply connected to a slow pace lifestyle, a meditative practice, often missing from our modern lives.

The basic idea is that the artwork can be in continuous change (like humanity). It can be applied to any space, be rolled, stretched and fitted in a suitcase. Human beings have always been moving for survival or progression. Memories, customs and traditions are their only heritage - House. In a shifting period the traditional patterns are not reproduced the same. Each one can ‘knit’ their own history.

Artists, craftsmen and other volunteers were invited to participate to the process of transforming the installation and become a part of the ''Continuous thread''. It has been presented as a scenographic piece in theatre and dance performances, it was worn as a garment at a dance performance, and exhibited in various other ways.

Ritewright | Nicosia Open Up Festival

Ritewright, performance, Nicosia Open Up Festival
19/10/2023, Gardens of the Future

"Ritewright" is a twenty-minute performance that is a process of creating ritual practices that arise from the need for tenderness and pleasure. The performer interacts with objects she has recreated from unwanted objects by readjusting them in a calm yet dynamic environment.

Coreto | Portuguese Basket Technology Summer Camp, 2019

Coreto - Junça and Baracejo Coffee table

How can basketry product create a more sustainable living by bringing nature into shared living?

In the past, basketry was mostly rural areas as a mean of storage or transportation in everyday tasks. Our challenge was to bring this humble material to a new context, adding value and making relevant for the twenty first century urban living.

Coreto is a coffee table that can be easily transformed from 2D to 3D. A flat pack furniture that can be easily put together without screws. It was also designed to fit in a check-in luggage.

Nowadays, many people live as “modern nomads” frequently changing home. The aim behind Coreto was to design an object that responds to the “modern nomads” needs of mobility without compromising on sustainable living.

Inter-Weaving | A Climate Challenge: Platform of Events by Phytorio, 2018

Basketry and straw weaving workshop with Alexandra Pambouka

The workshop Inter-Weaving proposed an artistic approach towards weaving with plants and also the simulation of these techniques with the human body. The participants came into contact with weaving techniques through experiential processes and exercises. Observing the weaving techniques, they had the chance to make their own deductions on what constitutes a team. At the end of the workshop, a presentation and an exhibition of participants’ work took place.
